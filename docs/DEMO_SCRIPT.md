# Inquiry: a three-minute VATT demo

Before the interview, deploy via the README, enter the demo code, and verify the live provider. Use only fictional input. Leave room for provider latency; never describe a failed or mocked call as live success.

This is a focused demo of the Value Add of Technology on Teaching (VATT): AI offers a second perspective, while the educator remains the author and decision maker. It demonstrates an AI integration interaction, not a complete PLC application or evidence of teaching outcomes.

## 0:00–0:30 · Evidence and human authorship

“This demonstrates VATT: AI can support educator thinking, but the educator owns the decision. The data is fictional.”

Point to October 28, four classrooms, 96 responses: 68/96 correct products; 29/96 adequate explanations. These are separate hypothetical rubric measures, with unknown overlap. Show the area model and work-C's procedural wording.

Edit Your Interpretation: “Some students may understand the area model but struggle to express why each overlapping cell is a hundredth.”

“Nothing calls AI until I ask.”

## 0:30–1:10 · Challenge, reject, then accept

Click **Challenge my interpretation**. Inspect the actual returned hypothesis, probing question, source chips, and uncertainty. Click a source chip to locate its dated fixture.

“This is a hypothesis to investigate, not a causal diagnosis.”

Click **Reject suggestion**. Point to the unchanged interpretation and inquiry; expand the rejected decision in the trail. “Rejecting is valid and does not block me.”

Return to **1 Challenge**, click **Try another challenge**, and inspect this fresh live response. Click **Accept suggestion**. “The proposed interpretation is copied into my editable draft; it is not confirmed.” Inspect or edit it, then click **Confirm interpretation and continue**.

## 1:10–1:45 · Refine, then confirm

Click **Suggest a stronger PLC question**—a separate live call. Read the proposed wording and limitations. Accept it if useful, or Reject and retain the draft. Make a small educator edit.

“AI acceptance is not confirmation. I own this wording.”

Click **Confirm inquiry**. Point to **Educator confirmed · Request context**.

## 1:45–2:30 · Rehearse one mathematical idea

Click **Suggest a PLC activity**—the third action, using only the confirmed inquiry and approved sources. Inspect one activity, its segments totaling 15 minutes, correct 12-hundredths representation, and facilitation details.

“This is a teacher rehearsal, not a whole meeting plan or a finalized classroom strategy.”

Click **Reject suggestion**, type a short educator-authored activity, and show it stays editable. If time permits, request another live activity and Reject to demonstrate the existing activity is preserved. Request again and **Accept suggestion** to show the copy-to-preview path. Click **Confirm activity** only after reviewing/editing it.

## 2:30–3:00 · Consequences and trust

Return to **2 Refine**, change the inquiry, and click **Confirm inquiry** again. Point to **Needs review** on the retained activity and the disabled confirmation. Review its alignment using **Review accepted activity**, or request a new rehearsal.

“The coach chooses what to bring to discussion; the team still decides the classroom change. Rejected AI is never silently reused as accepted context. Historical one-classroom observations are excluded, and we make no causal claims from these fixtures.”

Close on the decision trail. **Reset demo** clears session-local drafts for repetition.

## Complete all three steps without AI

1. Select **Use guided planning**. Compare Your Interpretation with the three evidence-testing prompts and nearby sources. Ask whether oral or visual reasoning could provide evidence not visible in writing; do not treat that possibility as a finding. Edit or keep the interpretation, then select **Continue with my interpretation**.
2. Keep the confirmed interpretation visible. Review the four inquiry checks and open **Sentence frame** if helpful. Write: “What would labeled grids and oral explanations reveal about how students understand the hundredth in 0.3 times 0.4?” Select **Confirm inquiry**.
3. Use that confirmed inquiry as the anchor. In **Activity preview**, write an objective (practice eliciting the unit of the overlap), a 3-minute grid construction, 7 minutes rehearsing questions and explanations, and 5 minutes comparing interpretations and choosing subsequent evidence. Include two questions: “What is the whole?” and “Why is each overlap cell a hundredth?” Name what teachers practice (neutral follow-ups) and subsequent evidence (labeled grids plus oral/written explanations). Review and select **Confirm activity**.

Nothing in this route requests AI or inserts prewritten prose. Confirmation brings the rehearsal to team discussion; it does not select a classroom intervention or establish effectiveness.

If a request returns no valid AI suggestion, the same built-in guide appears automatically and the educator continues with unchanged drafts. For a slow request, this happens at the eight-second UI waiting threshold, not as evidence that the provider failed. **Service details** remains collapsed. **Try AI** is optional; do not retry before continuing.
